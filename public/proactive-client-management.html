The Core Difference

Reactive

  • Wait for the client to notice problems
  • Respond to questions after they're asked
  • Fix issues when they become urgent
  • Let the client drive the agenda
  • Report what happened

Proactive

  • Flag problems before the client sees them
  • Anticipate questions and answer them first
  • Catch issues while they're still small
  • Come to calls with a point of view
  • Recommend what to do next

Reactive teams get managed. Proactive teams become trusted partners.

What Reactive Looks Like

Tracking breaks

You notice conversion tracking is broken on Wednesday. You wait to mention it on Friday's call. The client asks why their numbers look weird. You explain the issue. Client asks why they're just hearing about this now.

Performance dips

CPL has been rising for two weeks. You report the numbers on the weekly call without commentary. Client asks what's going on. You say you'll look into it. Client wonders why you weren't already looking into it.

Campaign changes

You pause a campaign on Monday because budget is running hot. Client's sales team notices leads dropped. Client emails asking what happened. You explain. Client asks why they weren't told first.

"Why am I just hearing about this now?"
If you hear this, you were reactive.

What Proactive Looks Like

Tracking breaks

You notice conversion tracking is broken on Wednesday. You Slack the client immediately: "Heads up, we found a tracking issue. Already working on a fix. Will confirm when resolved. Data from Wed-Thu may be incomplete." Client knows. No surprises on Friday.

Performance dips

CPL has been rising for two weeks. You come to the call with: "CPL is up 18% over the past two weeks. We think it's audience fatigue on the top performer. We're testing three new angles this week. Here's what we expect to see."

Campaign changes

Budget is running hot. Before pausing anything, you message the client: "We're pacing 15% over budget. I recommend pausing Campaign X for the rest of the week. This will reduce leads by roughly Y. Want to confirm before we make the change."

"Thanks for the heads up."
If you hear this, you were proactive.

The Proactive Habits

👀

Check Before They Do

Look at the data before the client does. If something is off, you should know first. Daily pacing checks. Weekly trend reviews. Catch it before it becomes a question.

🚨

Flag Early, Not Late

The moment you see something wrong, say something. A same-day Slack beats a next-week explanation. Clients forgive issues. They don't forgive being kept in the dark.

💡

Come With a POV

Don't just report numbers. Have an opinion. "Here's what's working. Here's what's not. Here's what I think we should do about it." Clients want a strategist, not a reporter.

🔮

Anticipate the Next Question

Before every call, ask yourself: What will they ask? What should they know? What would I want to know if I were them? Then answer those questions before they're asked.

Before Every Client Call

1

What changed?

Any tracking issues? Budget pacing problems? Performance swings? Campaigns paused or launched? Know it before they ask.

2

What's working?

What should we scale? What creative is winning? What audience is converting? Bring specific recommendations, not just observations.

3

What's not?

What should we pause or change? What's underperforming and why? What's your hypothesis? What's the fix?

4

What do they care about?

What are their business priorities right now? What metrics matter to their boss? What's keeping them up at night?

5

What will they ask?

Put yourself in their shoes. What questions would you have? Prepare the answers before they ask.

6

What should they know?

Even if they don't ask, what do they need to know? Industry trends? Competitor moves? Platform changes? Bring it.

The Communication Rules

⚡

Bad news travels fast

If something breaks, they hear about it today. Not Friday. Not "when we have more info." Today. With what you know and what you're doing about it.

🔔

Changes get a heads up

Before you pause a campaign, change budget, or shift strategy, the client knows first. They should never be surprised by something we did.

📊

Data gets context

Never send numbers without a story. What does this mean? Is it good or bad? What caused it? What are we doing about it? Numbers without context create anxiety.

🎯

Recommendations are specific

Not "we could try some new creative." Instead: "We recommend testing three new headline angles focused on [X]. Here's why. Here's the timeline. Here's what we expect."

The Trust Equation

Trust = Consistency + Transparency + Competence

Consistency

Same quality every time. Same communication cadence. Same preparation level. Clients trust patterns. Be predictable in a good way.

Transparency

No hiding bad news. No spinning numbers. No waiting to see if they notice. If something's wrong, say it. Honesty builds trust faster than perfection.

Competence

Know your stuff. Come prepared. Have answers. When you don't know, say so and follow up fast. Being proactive shows you're paying attention.

Proactive communication is how you demonstrate all three.
